# Copywriting methods: building a buyer avatar / ICP sheet and turning it into website copy (FainStudio, Czech SME owners)

Scope note: research done in October 2026. About 25 searches and fetches. Primary practitioner pages were opened where possible: Copyhackers, jobstobedone.org (JTBD Radio), the WSU press release and NN/g. These Gartner and CXL pages returned HTTP 403, so their figures come from search-result snippets of the official URLs and are flagged as such: the 2026 Gartner release, the gartner.com buying-journey page and the CXL VOC article. Several framework descriptions come from secondary summaries rather than the books themselves: Schwartz, Dunford, Hormozi and StoryBrand. Marked as **[evidence]** = data from a study or survey; **[practitioner opinion]** = a framework or expert view with no controlled data; **[inference]** = my synthesis for FainStudio.

## 1. What fields does a high-quality avatar/ICP sheet contain (per recognised practitioners), and which fields are useless?

### Takeaway
Practitioners agree that a useful avatar for copy is a **decision record**, not a demographic portrait. It needs:
- the trigger and the struggle (push)
- the outcome they picture (pull)
- the fears (anxiety) and current workaround (habit)
- what they'd do instead (competitive alternatives)
- their awareness and sophistication level
- what they need to believe the promise (proof)
- their own words

Demographics, hobbies, a stock photo and a fictional name don't change a single headline, so they are filler.

### Cited Findings
**JTBD, "Forces of Progress" (Moesta / Spiek, The Re-Wired Group)** [practitioner opinion, based on qualitative switch interviews]
- A switch happens only when **Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit**. The four forces are:
  - **push**: frustration with the current situation
  - **pull**: the better life the customer pictures, which the JTBD site stresses "is not your feature list"
  - **anxiety**: "What if it doesn't work? What if I look foolish? What about my data?"
  - **habit**: the inertia of the status quo
  — [jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/?p=3423) (via search summary); [JTBD Radio: Pull and Anxiety](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- Moesta: "There's really never not a push." Without a situation to connect to, a product's appeal goes unnoticed. — [JTBD Radio: Pull and Anxiety](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- Moesta calls anxiety a "hidden gem". Teams wrongly assume "If I add more features and benefits people will like it more". Spiek says anxiety "is probably one of the most overlooked aspects on the Web." — [JTBD Radio: Pull and Anxiety](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- Habit of the present = "the way you have always done things"; "if you don't speak to what I'm already doing, you're going to lose." — [JTBD Radio: Habit of the Present](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/forces-friday-habit-of-the-present/)
- How the forces are mapped: switch interviews rebuild the timeline of the decision. They ask about the first moment the customer realised something had to change, what they tried, and what they feared. Recency matters because memory degrades. Job statement format: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]." — [JTBD search summary incl. jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/?p=3423) (secondary wording)

**April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (positioning components, in order)** [practitioner opinion]
1. **Competitive alternatives**: what customers would do if you didn't exist. Often "a spreadsheet or nothing", not a named rival.
2. **Unique attributes**: what you can do that the alternatives can't.
3. **Value**, with proof.
4. **Best-fit customers**: the segment that cares most about that value.
5. **Market category** / frame of reference.
6. Optional: **relevant trends**.

Order matters, and best-fit customers often reveal the right frame. — [Heinz Marketing summary of Obviously Awesome](https://www.heinzmarketing.com/blog/five-components-of-effective-positioning-an-obviously-awesome-book-summary-part-2/) (secondary summary)

**Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966)** [practitioner opinion]
- **Awareness level**: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most aware. The stage determines "your headline, your opening hook, your proof points, your call to action." — [Leadgen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/five-stages-awareness-lead-generation/); [Adbeat](https://blog.adbeat.com/5-levels-customer-awareness/) (secondary)
- **Market sophistication**: how many claims the market has already heard. In summaries it is explicitly "not about demographics or psychographics". The stages run:
  1. a simple claim
  2. an enlarged claim
  3. a **unique mechanism** ("the 'how' behind the 'what'") once the market is skeptical
  4. an enlarged or more specific mechanism
  5. identification / identity
  
  Note: secondary sources disagree on whether the mechanism is stage 3 or 4. — [Taylor Pearson](https://taylorpearson.me/?p=11932); search summary of Schwartz summaries

**Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*, Value Equation** [practitioner heuristic, not measurable]
- Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice).
- "Perceived" likelihood is raised by proof, testimonials and guarantees. Time delay is cut by quick wins. Effort is cut by describing how you make it easier. — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/); [Medium](https://medium.com/illumination/alex-hormozis-value-equation-is-a-true-revelation-b887363cc6b6) (secondary)

**Donald Miller, StoryBrand SB7** [practitioner opinion]
- Problem in three layers:
  - **external**: tangible
  - **internal**: how it makes the customer feel. "Companies sell solutions to external problems, but customers buy solutions to internal ones."
  - **philosophical**: why it's wrong
- Plus a simple 3–4 step **plan**, a CTA, and explicit **failure stakes** (what happens if they don't act) vs. success. — [Graham Mann book notes](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller); [Leadgen Economy on StoryBrand for B2B](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/storybrand-framework-b2b-lead-gen-ecommerce-funnels/) (secondary)

**Copyhackers / Joanna Wiebe** [practitioner opinion]
- Messaging should come from prospects' own words, not from the writer's head. Review mining data "should drive your copy, not your own guesses." — [Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/?p=9003) (search summary)
- Review mining sorts quotes into six buckets:
  1. Problems in Life
  2. Problems with Solutions ("the specific solutions people hire instead of choosing your product or service")
  3. Motivations to Seek You Out
  4. Desires that Drive Purchase
  5. Specific Features
  6. Anxieties and Hesitations
  
  — [Copyhackers: rapid-fire review mining](https://copyhackers.com/how-to-do-rapid-fire-review-mining/)
- Wiebe's 2026 book *The Copyselling System* promises to "pinpoint your core message by listening to new leads and customers." It uses a "message matrix" to match message to stage and channel. — [BenBella Books](https://benbellabooks.com/shop/the-copyselling-system/)

**Gartner (B2B buying)** [evidence, survey; enterprise-skewed samples]
- Buyers spend about **17% of the total purchase journey meeting potential suppliers**, which works out to 5–6% with any one rep when they compare several. — [Growth Method summary of Gartner](https://growthmethod.com/gartner-b2b-buying-journey/) (secondary; the gartner.com page returned 403)

### Inferences
**[inference] Proposed one-page A4 avatar sheet for FainStudio.** Each field is something copy can directly use:
1. **Who exactly (best-fit, not demographic).** Owner or managing director of a Czech firm with roughly 5–50 people who personally handles or approves inquiries, invoices and price lists. One person decides and signs; no buying committee. Name the 3–4 best-fit sectors.
2. **Trigger moment (push).** The concrete situation in their own words that made the problem urgent. Examples: "an inquiry sat for two days and the client went elsewhere"; "the bookkeeper is drowning at month-end."
3. **Job statement.** "When ___, I want ___, so I can ___", one line per service.
4. **Competitive alternatives / habit.** What they do today: themselves, an assistant, Excel, a nephew, an agency, a SaaS tool they half-use, or nothing. What they'd lose by switching.
5. **Pull / dream outcome.** The "after" picture in their words: hours back, faster reply, fewer errors, ability to grow without hiring.
6. **Top 5 anxieties and objections, ranked**, each paired with the planned answer (guarantee, proof, process step, price fact). For example:
   - "it won't work for our case"
   - "it'll take months and my time"
   - "I'll pay and be stuck"
   - "AI makes mistakes"
   - "what about our data / GDPR"
   - "a small studio will vanish"
7. **Awareness level on arrival** (by source: cold call vs. referral) and **market sophistication** (what AI/automation claims they've already heard).
8. **Proof they'll believe.** Hierarchy: a named Czech local client with a number, then a demo or video of the actual workflow, then the founder's face, name and phone, then process facts.
9. **Their words: a VOC phrase bank.** 10–15 verbatim phrases from calls, with words to use and words to avoid.
10. **Decision mechanics.** How they buy (a phone call, on a phone, in under 2 minutes), what they must see before booking 20 minutes, the price anchor they compare to (an employee's monthly cost, an agency quote), and who else they'll ask (accountant, spouse or partner).
11. **One-sentence positioning** (Dunford). For [best-fit], who today [alternative], FainStudio [unique attribute], which means [value], proven by [proof].

**[inference] Useless or low-value fields:** age, gender, hobbies, favourite brands, a stock photo, a cute persona name, "personality type", and generic "goals: grow the business". None of them changes a headline, an objection answer or a CTA. Schwartz summaries state that sophistication and awareness are "not about demographics or psychographics". JTBD's model rests on the situation and forces, not on attributes.

**[inference] Caveat on Gartner.** Its "buying group" research targets mid and large enterprise purchases. A Czech SME owner usually decides alone or with one adviser, so the consensus-building material matters less than the independent-validation material.

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary, quotable statement from Wiebe or Moesta explicitly calling demographic personas useless. The "useless fields" list is my synthesis from the frameworks' logic, not a direct quote. Moesta's often-cited line that demographics don't cause buying was not verified in this session.
- Primary text of Dunford, Schwartz, Hormozi and Miller was not accessed; all framework descriptions are from secondary summaries.

## 2. How is Voice of Customer (VOC) research done, and how do pros turn it into headlines and objection handling?

### Takeaway
Pros collect customers' verbatim language from several sources, tag it by force or theme, and then lift exact phrases into headlines, subheads and objection answers. Interviews and calls with real leads give the best, most unfiltered language. Review mining is the fast substitute. For FainStudio, the founder's cold calls are themselves the best VOC source.

### Cited Findings
**Methods Copyhackers lists for pre-writing research** [practitioner opinion] — [Copyhackers: copywriting research](https://copyhackers.com/2022/06/copywriting-research/) (Jessica Noel, ed. Joanna Wiebe, 2022 with 2025 updates):
- Interviews with clients, teams, customers and ex-customers ("It's the #1 best way to get unfiltered language", Kristi Durham).
- Visitor and customer surveys. They reveal use scenarios, outcomes, objections and the people who influenced the purchase.
- Review mining on Amazon, Reddit, TripAdvisor and Yelp.
- Heat and scroll maps.
- Thank-you polls after opt-in or purchase, asking what led them to you.
- Analytics.
- Competitor audits: "How are they addressing customer objections?"

**Other method details**
- Wiebe calls synthesis "that messy 90% of the work". Research defines both the messaging hierarchy (the order of messages) and the words used. — [Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2022/06/copywriting-research/)
- Review mining technique: Google `site:amazon.com inurl:"product-reviews" "tired of" ~keyword`, then vary with "frustrated by", "wanting" and feature names. — [Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/how-to-do-rapid-fire-review-mining/)
- For services, mine reviews of books and products "hired for the same job" your service would be hired for. Review mining avoids the interviewer bias of interrupting or filtering the person. — Copyhackers posts surfaced in search ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/?p=22133); the exact page was not opened)

**Headline proof point** [evidence, but a single uncontrolled-for-generalisation case]
- Wiebe wrote a rehab-centre headline lifted verbatim from an Amazon book review on addiction. It reportedly produced **>400% more clicks** on the main CTA and **>20% more form submissions** on the next page. — [CXL: Voice of Customer](https://cxl.com/blog/voice-of-customer/) (via search snippet; page returned 403)

**JTBD interview technique** [practitioner opinion]
- Rebuild the timeline (first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding) and dig past vague praise. "Looks great" should be probed until it becomes something like "my shoulder won't hurt". — [JTBD Radio: Pull and Anxiety](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- "Find out where the anxiety is." — Moesta, [same source](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- Advertising should show "the moments that you aren't aware of that you have all the time." — Moesta, [same source](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)

**Objection handling from VOC** [practitioner opinion]
- Habit must be named explicitly: "if you don't call them out, they come out anyways", as hidden friction. — [JTBD Radio: Habit of the Present](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/forces-friday-habit-of-the-present/)

### Inferences
**[inference] A practical VOC process for FainStudio in 1–2 weeks:**
1. **Founder's cold-call log.** After every call, write down verbatim:
   - the first objection
   - how they describe their current process
   - the words they use for the problem (likely "poptávky", "faktury", "papíry", "Excel", "nestíhám", "ztrácíme zakázky")
   - the reason given for "no" or "not now"

   Twenty calls gives a usable phrase bank.
2. **Five switch interviews with current or past clients.** 20 minutes each. Ask: what was happening when you decided to deal with this? What did you try before? What almost stopped you? What did you tell your partner or accountant?
3. **Review mining (Czech).** Firmy.cz and Google reviews of competing agencies and freelancers. Reviews of SaaS tools SMEs half-use (invoice OCR, chatbots, CRM). Czech owner forums and Facebook groups ("podnikatelé", e-shop owner groups). Search phrases like "nestíhám odpovídat", "ruční přepisování faktur".
4. **Tag** each quote into: push, pull, anxiety, habit, alternative, proof wanted.
5. **Turn quotes into copy:**
   - the most frequent **push** phrase becomes the H1 or problem line
   - the most frequent **pull** phrase becomes the subhead or outcome
   - the top 5 **anxieties** become the FAQ or "what if" block, in the customer's wording
   - **habit/alternative** becomes the comparison section (vs. doing it yourself / hiring / SaaS)

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found quantifying VOC-derived copy vs. writer-generated copy beyond single case studies like the rehab headline. Treat the uplift as anecdotal.
- No Czech-specific VOC sources were researched; that belongs to adjacent research.
- No primary Copyhackers guidance on using **sales-call transcripts** specifically was retrieved, though interviews with leads are endorsed.

## 3. How do pros handle the anxiety and habit forces and objections for a risky purchase from a small or unknown vendor?

### Takeaway
More features or benefits (pull) does not fix anxiety; it can raise it. Pros reduce anxiety directly:
- guarantees and reversibility
- trials or samples
- taking over the hard part
- expert reassurance
- concrete, verifiable proof

They also make switching easier against habit, by speaking to "what I'm already doing". For B2B in 2025–2026, the evidence says buyers are increasingly skeptical and want verifiable proof, not promises.

### Cited Findings
**JTBD on anxiety and habit** [practitioner opinion]
- "Sometimes increasing the pull actually increases the anxiety." When the two rise equally, "they negate each other." — Moesta, [JTBD Radio: Pull and Anxiety](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- Anxiety reducers named in that episode — [same source](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/):
  - money-back guarantees and easy returns ("I knew I could just return it")
  - free trial or "30 day sample"
  - including the hard part as a standard service ("it was us managing the anxiety that helped improve sales")
  - expert opinion, which reduces the anxiety "of 'I don't want to make the wrong decision'"
  - not asking "the hardest question up front"
  - addressing the fear of not knowing how to use it
- Real deadlines reduce anxiety more than "every day low prices", per Spiek. — [same source](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- On habit — [JTBD Radio: Habit of the Present](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/forces-friday-habit-of-the-present/):
  - name what customers already do and what they'd give up
  - reduce the social and practical cost of switching
  - target moments when the old way breaks ("a moment of unhooking")

**Hormozi** [heuristic]
- Raise "perceived likelihood of achievement" with proof and guarantees; cut time delay and effort. — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)

**StoryBrand** [practitioner opinion]
- Give a simple 3–4 step plan that "shows customers how easy it is to do business with you", and state the failure stakes. — [Leadgen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/storybrand-framework-b2b-lead-gen-ecommerce-funnels/); [Graham Mann](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller)

**B2B evidence on proof** [evidence, survey]
- Forrester's Buyers' Journey Survey 2025: B2B buyers are becoming more skeptical of AI-generated claims and moving toward evidence-backed validation. 19% said they were less confident in purchase decisions because AI produced inaccurate or misleading results. — [Digital Commerce 360 on Forrester](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/10/28/forrester-b2b-buyers-demand-proof-not-promises/)
- TrustRadius, January 2026 (1,862 tech buyers, 444 vendors) — [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/business-services/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-outputs-and-vendors-are-underestimating-how-far-trust-has-fallen); [TrustRadius report page](https://research.agilebrandguide.com/reports/trustradius/trustradius-bridging-the-b2b-buying-disconnect-ai-trust-and-go-to-market-strategies-for-2026):
  - vendor marketing collateral ranked **last** among the resources buyers actually consult
  - 94% of AI-using buyers fact-check AI outputs at least sometimes
  - the report recommends claims buyers can verify

**Pricing transparency as anxiety reduction** [evidence, usability testing, older]
- NN/g: price is one of the first things users research. Hidden B2B prices were the "#1 Web design mistake of 2002". For custom services, show "representative cases and their prices". At the shortlisting stage the site's job is to survive the cut, not close. — [NN/g: Show Prices for Common Scenarios (2006)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)
- NN/g topic page: price is prospects' #1 information need, and test participants went to competitors when prices weren't shown. — [NN/g B2B topic](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/b2b-websites/) (via search summary)
- Demand Gen Report / Demandbase 2021 (n=257 B2B execs): 65% say easy access to pricing and competitive information is among what they look for on a vendor's site. — [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2021/45409/what-b2b-buyers-want-from-vendor-websites)

### Inferences
**[inference] FainStudio already has unusually strong risk-reversal material.** It should be presented as a single "what happens if it doesn't work" block, not scattered:
- one-off price, no subscription (removes the "trapped" fear)
- live within 10 days (time delay)
- 14 days of tuning included
- balance paid only after handover (a built-in guarantee)
- a 20-minute call as a low-commitment first step

Map each to a named anxiety:
- "Balance only after handover" answers "I'll pay and get nothing."
- "No subscription, it's yours" answers lock-in.
- "Live in 10 days, we do the setup" answers "it'll eat my time" (the effort/sacrifice term in Hormozi's equation).
- "14 days of tuning" answers "AI makes mistakes / it won't fit our case."

**[inference] Proof hierarchy for a small, unknown vendor**, strongest first:
1. A named local client with a specific before/after number ("odpovídáme do 5 minut místo do 2 dnů")
2. A short screen-recording of the actual workflow on a realistic document or inquiry
3. A worked price example per service (NN/g "representative cases": e.g. "automatické zpracování faktur pro firmu s ~200 fakturami/měsíc: od 28 000 Kč")
4. A visible founder: real name, face and phone (lowers the "small studio will vanish" anxiety)
5. Process steps with dates
6. Generic claims, which are weakest and best avoided

**[inference] Answer "what if it fails" explicitly in copy.** Say what happens if the automation misreads a document (a human check, error flagging, the tuning period), and that the client owns the output and data. This targets the specific fears JTBD says to surface ("What about my data?").

**[inference] Habit against "I'll just keep doing it by hand / my assistant does it".** The copy should say what doesn't change: they keep their Excel, email and accounting software, and the automation feeds into them. That matches the "speak to what I'm already doing" principle.

### Gaps
- No controlled B2B study quantifying the conversion effect of guarantees or "pay after delivery" for small service vendors was found. The advice is practitioner consensus, not measured.
- No quantitative data was found on how much a named founder or face increases trust for B2B SME services specifically.
- Czech-specific attitudes to GDPR and data concerns with AI vendors were not researched.

## 4. How does awareness level change what the page should say, and which level is typical after a cold call or a referral?

### Takeaway
The less aware the visitor, the more the page must lead with the problem and the situation. The more aware, the more it should lead with the offer, proof and CTA. A prospect who just had a cold call or a referral is no longer unaware: they have heard of FainStudio and roughly what it does. The page's job is to **verify and de-risk** fast on a phone, not to educate from zero.

### Cited Findings
**Schwartz's five stages and what each needs on the page** [practitioner opinion]
- Unaware → create awareness of the problem
- Problem-aware → show the solution category
- Solution-aware → show why this solution is the one
- Product-aware → overcome hesitation with proof and offer
- Most aware → a direct offer and CTA
- The stage "determines everything – your headline, your opening hook, your proof points, your call to action". A page for unaware visitors leads with the problem; for most-aware visitors it can lead with a direct CTA.

— [Leadgen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/five-stages-awareness-lead-generation/); [Adbeat](https://blog.adbeat.com/5-levels-customer-awareness/) (secondary summaries)

**Market sophistication** [practitioner opinion]
- When a market has heard all the claims and grows skeptical, a bigger promise stops working. You need a **unique mechanism**, the "how", or more specificity. — [Taylor Pearson](https://taylorpearson.me/?p=11932) (secondary)

**Buyers prefer to validate on their own** [evidence, survey]
- Gartner, June 2025 (n=632 B2B buyers, fielded August–September 2024): **61%** prefer a rep-free buying experience, and **73%** actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach. — [Gartner press release, 25 June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience) (via search snippet)
- Gartner, March 2026 (n=646, fielded August–September 2025): **67%** prefer rep-free, and **45%** used AI during a recent purchase. Gartner analyst: sellers can't rely on static collateral when buyers do key tasks on their own. — [Gartner press release, 9 March 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) (via search snippet; fetch blocked)
- Gartner, May 2026: **69%** of B2B buyers turn to sales reps to validate AI-generated insights. — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260520585188/en/Gartner-Survey-Finds-69-of-B2B-Buyers-Turn-to-Sales-Reps-to-Validate-AI-Generated-Insights) (via search snippet)

**The website's role at this stage** [evidence-based guidance, older]
- NN/g: at the shortlisting stage the site's goal is to survive the shortlist cut. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)

### Inferences
**[inference] After a cold call: product-aware but skeptical.** The visitor knows the name and has heard a pitch. They are often only weakly problem-aware ("we manage somehow"), with low trust: Gartner says 73% avoid suppliers with irrelevant outreach. They open the site to check two things: is this real and competent, and is it relevant to my firm? In the first screen they need to see:
- what exactly you do, in plain words that match the call
- for whom
- the price level
- proof of real people and real clients
- the booking CTA

The problem framing still matters, because the push may be weak. Lead with the concrete situation they recognise, not with "AI".

**[inference] After a referral: product-aware to most aware, with borrowed trust.** The page should confirm the referrer's story fast: what was built for a similar firm, price, timeline and the booking link. Less persuasion, more confirmation.

**[inference] Recommended page order for both entry routes on mobile:**
1. Plain-language H1 (outcome + for whom)
2. A subline with price, timing and payment-after-handover
3. The CTA (book 20 minutes)
4. 3–6 concrete use cases as "situations" (push language)
5. Proof (client plus number, demo)
6. How it works in 3–4 steps (plan)
7. Price examples
8. A "what if it doesn't work" FAQ
9. The founder
10. The CTA repeated

This is a product-aware layout with a short problem-aware bridge.

**[inference] Sophistication.** By 2026, Czech SME owners have likely heard many generic "AI will save you time" pitches, a stage-3-plus market. Bigger claims ("ušetříme 80 % času") will hit skepticism. Specificity (exact document types, the exact tools it feeds into, days to go live) and a visible "how" work better than bigger promises. The Forrester and TrustRadius findings on verifiable claims are consistent with this.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures the awareness level of cold-called B2B prospects; the classification above is reasoning.
- The Gartner samples are mostly larger B2B organisations. No Czech or SME-specific data on rep-free preference was found.

## 5. Common mistakes in B2B/AI-service copy, and what evidence says about how buyers react to "AI" wording

### Takeaway
The main documented risk is that naming "AI" can **lower** purchase intention by reducing emotional trust. The effect is stronger when the purchase feels risky, and a one-off spend of 18–45k CZK on business processes is risky for an SME. Practitioners and the study authors converge on the same advice: describe the concrete outcome and features, avoid AI buzzwords and inflated claims, show prices, and make claims verifiable.

### Cited Findings
**WSU study** [evidence, controlled experiments; consumers, not B2B]
- Cicek, Gursoy and Lu, *Journal of Hospitality Marketing & Management* (2024). Experimental surveys with more than 1,000 US adults.
- Product descriptions that mentioned "artificial intelligence" lowered purchase intention. In one test, identical smart-TV descriptions differed only by the term, and the group that saw it was less likely to buy.
- Mechanism: lower **emotional trust**.
- The effect was stronger for **high-risk** products: expensive electronics, medical devices, financial services.
- Tested across eight product and service categories.
- Cicek: "Focus on describing the features or benefits and avoid the AI buzzwords"; "Emphasizing AI may not always be beneficial, particularly for high-risk products."

— [WSU press release, 30 July 2024](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2024/07/30/using-the-term-artificial-intelligence-in-product-descriptions-reduces-purchase-intentions/). A trade summary reports "six experiments" vs. WSU's "eight categories"; the original paper (DOI 10.1080/19368623.2024.2368040) was not opened.

**AI-washing and B2B skepticism**
- G2 defines **AI-washing** as "overplaying or exaggerating the AI capabilities of offerings to lure buyers". — [G2 2024 Buyer Behavior Report](https://learn.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf) (via search summary)
- B2B buyers are increasingly skeptical of AI-generated claims and shift to evidence-backed validation. — [Forrester via Digital Commerce 360, October 2025](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/10/28/forrester-b2b-buyers-demand-proof-not-promises/) [evidence, survey]
- TrustRadius 2026, among AI-using buyers: 60% trust AI outputs only sometimes, 20% very often, 2% always. Vendor marketing collateral ranked last among resources consulted. — [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/business-services/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-outputs-and-vendors-are-underestimating-how-far-trust-has-fallen) [evidence, survey of tech buyers]

**Other documented mistakes**
- Hidden or obscured pricing. B2B users succeeded on tasks only **58%** of the time vs. 66% on consumer e-commerce sites (NN/g B2B research, 2006), with pushy marketing tactics causing "annoyance and distrust". — [DestinationCRM on NN/g](https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-News/CRM-Featured-Articles/B2B-Web-Sites-Are-Dropping-the-Ball-42449.aspx) (older, secondary); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)
- Feature lists in place of the pictured outcome: JTBD says pull "is not your feature list". — [jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/?p=3423)
- Piling on benefits without addressing anxiety. — [JTBD Radio](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)
- "Customers don't buy features; they buy the value those features deliver." — [Heinz Marketing on Dunford](https://www.heinzmarketing.com/blog/five-components-of-effective-positioning-an-obviously-awesome-book-summary-part-2/)
- Unclear category in the headline. In a Wynter-hosted message test of wynter.com, 4 of 15 (simulated) respondents could not place the product on first read, and the fix was to name the category earlier. — [Wynter grader report](https://grader.wynter.com/r/wynter-on-demand-messaging-and-market-research-vFnK2zE) (low evidential weight: AI-simulated personas)

### Inferences
**[inference] Treat "AI" as a mechanism detail, not the headline.** Lead with the job done ("Poptávky vyřízené do 5 minut, i v noci"; "Faktury se samy přepíšou do vaší tabulky") and mention AI lower down, as the "how". This applies the WSU finding (avoid AI in the lead claim for risky purchases) and Schwartz's sophistication logic (the mechanism as the credible "how"). The 2024 WSU evidence is from US consumers, so its transfer to Czech SME owners is plausible but unproven.

**[inference] Words to avoid or replace:**
| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "AI-powered" / "umělá inteligence" in H1 | the concrete job |
| "revoluční" | specific outcome |
| "digitální transformace" | the document type, the tool name |
| "chytré řešení" | "přepíše fakturu do Excelu" |
| "škálovatelné", "na míru" without a price | the price |
| "agentní AI" / "LLM" / "RAG" | "asistent, který odpovídá z vašeho ceníku" |
| inflated percentages | a real client's before/after |

**[inference] Specific risks in FainStudio's six-service list:**
- Six unrelated services (automation, documents, chat, scraping, landing pages, AI video) dilute the positioning, a Dunford-style best-fit and category problem.
- On a phone they read as a feature list.
- Better: group them under one clear category ("we take routine office work off your plate") with 3–4 situation-led cards. Keep landing pages and AI video secondary, or on a separate page.

**[inference] Show the price on the page.** FainStudio's "from 18 000 CZK, typically 28–45k, no subscription" is a competitive asset. NN/g's "representative cases" format fits exactly.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found testing "AI" wording specifically on **B2B or SME** buyers; the WSU evidence is consumer-side.
- No Czech-language evidence was found on how Czech SME owners react to "AI" or "umělá inteligence" wording. This would be a good target for the founder's own call notes or a quick message test.
- No independent, methodologically documented Wynter study on B2B jargon was found. Wynter's public material is product marketing or AI-simulated.
